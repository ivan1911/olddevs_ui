# Glossary of `@olddevs/ui`

Kit terms. One concept, one word: a synonym from a neighboring system (plugin, service, node,
library) introduces a second meaning, and the two drift apart silently. If a word has two
meanings, both get compound names ("palette primitive", "overlay layer"), and the bare word stays
with one of them.

A concept that came from the industry is called by the word of its origin: the Tiptap engine,
WAI-ARIA, React Aria, Radix, Material 3, Storybook, Node.js. The English term is taken from there,
and the Russian one is translated from the English, not the other way around: translating back from
Russian returns a different word, and the reader cannot find the concept by searching. The kit
coins its own name only for a concept that the industry has no name for.

Every table has two columns for this rule. "Source" is where the English word comes from: the
origin, "common" (an ordinary industry word with no single owner) or "own" (the kit's name). "Also
searched as" lists the words other systems use for the same concept: they help a search find the
row, and they are not used in prose.

A public name (a prop, a function, a type) comes from the English term. Renaming follows semver
(the "Versions" section of the kit's `AGENTS.md`): the new name arrives as an alias in a minor
version, and the old one is marked `@deprecated` and removed in the next major version.

Origins: [Tiptap](https://tiptap.dev/docs), [ProseMirror](https://prosemirror.net/docs/guide/),
[WAI-ARIA APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/), [React Aria](https://react-spectrum.adobe.com/react-aria/),
[Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com), [M3](https://m3.material.io),
[DTCG](https://www.designtokens.org) (the W3C token format), [Storybook](https://storybook.js.org),
[Chromatic](https://www.chromatic.com/docs/), [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/api/packages.html),
[MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org), [BlockNote](https://www.blocknotejs.org),
[Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com), [MUI](https://mui.com), [Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com),
[Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/),
[CKEditor](https://ckeditor.com/docs/ckeditor5/latest/features/source-editing.html),
[Astro](https://docs.astro.build/en/concepts/islands/),
[text-mask](https://github.com/text-mask/text-mask/blob/master/componentDocumentation.md#guide),
[Ben Kamens](https://bjk5.com/post/44698559168/breaking-down-amazons-mega-dropdown) (menu aim).

General terms are written in prose as ordinary words, lowercase: "consumer", "role", "showcase".
Editor terms are capitalized ("Block", "Toolbar"): they are easy to confuse with an everyday word,
and the capital letter shows that the word means exactly what the table says.

A new term enters this glossary in the same change in which it appears. The glossary lives in the
package, not in the documentation repository, because it changes together with the code; the
documentation site shows it from the pinned kit version.

Prop names are a separate vocabulary:
[semantic prop vocabulary](conventions.md#semantic-prop-vocabulary).

| **axis**       | An independent prop with a closed set of values shared across the whole kit: `size`, `tone`, `variant`. An axis is turned on by an explicit prop                     | A state (`selected`, `disabled`, `loading`): it is boolean and named positively | own    | —                |
| **variant**    | The `variant` axis: how prominent the tone is — `solid`, `soft`, `outline`, `ghost`, one set across the kit                                                          | The appearance (`appearance`)                                                   | Radix  | —                |

## Tiptap

Words of the [Tiptap](https://tiptap.dev) engine that the Editor is built on. They are kept as they
are in Tiptap's documentation: a synonym from another editor (plugin, service) would introduce a
second meaning next to Block and Tool. The author of a custom Tool writes Extensions in Tiptap
and reads Tiptap's documentation — the table connects the engine's word to the kit's. In the "Meaning"
column, first what the word means in Tiptap, then what corresponds to it in the kit.
